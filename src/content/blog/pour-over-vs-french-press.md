Pour-over and French press start from the same beans and end up in completely different places. The difference isn't really about equipment quality — it's about what stays in the cup.

## Filtration is the real variable

A paper filter in a pour-over catches most of the oils and fine sediment. A French press's metal mesh lets both through. That's why French press coffee feels heavier and rounder, while pour-over tends to taste brighter and more defined.

## Which one should you use?

If you want clarity — distinct notes, a lighter body — go pour-over. If you want body and richness, French press wins. Neither is "better"; they're answering different questions about the same beans.

## What's happening at the chemistry level

Coffee oils carry a lot of the aromatic compounds that give a cup its character — florals, fruit notes, that kind of thing. A paper filter traps most of these along with the fine grounds. That's a trade: you lose some of the aromatic oil, but you also lose bitterness-contributing sediment, which is why pour-over tends to taste "cleaner."

A metal mesh filter, by contrast, has holes roughly ten times larger than paper's pores. Oils pass straight through, and so does a fine layer of grounds — the slight silty texture you notice at the bottom of a French press cup is exactly that.

## Temperature matters more than people think

Neither method forgives water that's too hot. Water above 205°F (96°C) pulls out compounds that read as bitter and astringent, regardless of filter type. Both pour-over and French press benefit from water in the 195–205°F range, ideally just off a rolling boil rather than straight out of it.

## Grind size, briefly

Pour-over generally wants a medium grind, closer to table salt in texture. French press wants a much coarser grind — closer to breadcrumbs — since the metal filter can't catch fine particles the way paper can. Getting this wrong is the single most common reason a French press cup tastes muddy: too fine a grind for a mesh filter means way too much sediment escaping into the cup.

## A simple way to decide

If you're brewing for yourself on a weekday morning and want speed and clarity, pour-over. If you're making a full pot for a slower Sunday and want something rounder and heavier, French press. Try both with the exact same beans once, back to back, and the difference becomes obvious in a way no amount of reading about it will get you to.
